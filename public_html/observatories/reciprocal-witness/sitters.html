How the two heart clocks synchronize

The dot on each sitter’s heart ring reaches the top on a beat. Both heartbeat strips share a five-second time window. These are simulated phase clocks. Openness is a switch in their equations. Each unsynchronized clock advances at its chosen resting rate. When both are open and the shortest phase gap lies inside the capture window, the leading clock slows and the trailing clock speeds up.

δ = wrap(θB − θA)
θ̇A = ωA + K g sin δ
θ̇B = ωB − K g sin δ

g is one when both gates are open and |δ| is within the window. Otherwise it is zero. K = 2π·5/60 radians/s. A stable captured gap satisfies sin δ* = (bpmB − bpmA)/10 and must lie within the window. Different resting rates can lock to a common frequency with a small beat lag. A closed pair can cross zero briefly as its phases drift.

The lock indicator requires six continuous model seconds within 1° of the predicted stable gap and within 0.10 bpm of a common frequency. In the far preset two equal-rate clocks start 140° apart. With different rates an initially distant pair can drift into range later. This exhibit applies an explicit feedback law to synthetic phases.
