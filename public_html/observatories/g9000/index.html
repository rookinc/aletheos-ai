FROM THE PAIR TO THE FIELD

900 states.
9,000 lifted states.
450 native darts.

The paired construction is G900 = (G60 □ G60) / diag D, with D = {1, a, b, ab}. It starts with 60 × 60 ordered pairs and identifies each shared four-element deck orbit. This gives 900 vertices and 3,600 edges, with four neighbors from each factor.

G1800 = (G60 □ G60) / diag {1, a} retains two lifts of each G900 state. Five frames give G9000. Every state has eight intra-frame neighbors plus F and F⁻¹, for 45,000 undirected edges and degree 10.

The full Thalean flower

The Flower view reuses the existing Kalachakra coordinate functions: 19 equal circles on a triangular lattice, opposite travel directions on alternating routes, and three XY/XZ/YZ drawing copies per route. All 450 routes and their 1,350 moving corners are present. D0 here is F1 in the original observer. Every K entry and every route state matches by identity.

Each original local point (u,v,w) maps by inverse stereographic projection, with a = 0.84 and r = 1 + w:

q = r · (2au, 2av, u² + v² − a²) / (a² + u² + v²)

The original plane mapping then places q into XY, XZ or YZ. At full wrap this preserves circles and their intersections within each chart and bloom-depth layer on concentric spheres. Intersections between different charts are not generally preserved. Depth zero gives one common unit sphere. The wrap slider compares the original three-plane coordinates with the spherical drawing; intermediate blends are display morphs. The live radius, latitude and longitude follow the selected copy. The original flower's continuous phase law is evaluated before projection, so its circles are retained throughout motion. Triangle connectors are geometric arcs, not added graph edges.

Nonconstant motion and the white projection

The Light view removes the invariant cycle mean m = (1/20)Σ pⱼ from each route's fully wrapped flower position. The residual q(t) = p(t) − m contains the nonconstant modes of this 20-step coordinate signal. This is mean removal for the F cycle, rather than a classification of all G9000 group representations. Interior positions are drawn at ρq/max(1,‖q‖), with ρ below one.

Residual directions, tail opacity and enabled RGB channels form a 72 × 36 angular field on the unit sphere. After a small angular smoothing kernel, the common white part in each cell is W = min(R,G,B). The remaining channels are (R−W,G−W,B−W). Colored residual traces stay inside and only W appears on the outer shell. The field is computed in model coordinates before the camera projection. Static circle guides and the removed cycle mean do not feed this projection. The white surface uses cell-area normalization and a display exposure curve; it is a specified visualization, not a derived physical radiation law.

Full-cycle dart tails

Each tail covers the previous 20 F steps, beginning at the dart. The gradient follows age from newest to oldest. At 0% the tail is hidden; at 50% its first step is opaque and its last step transparent; at 100% every step is opaque. Below 50% the first-step opacity rises from zero to full opacity. Above 50% the last-step opacity rises from zero to full opacity. Intermediate steps interpolate between these endpoints. The selected path uses this gradient while tails are visible; the separate selected orbit remains available at 0%.

RGB light and CMYK ink

RGB assigns red, green and blue to the XY, XZ and YZ copies. Their rendered traces add in screen color space toward white, with adjustable intensity and an optional glow. CMYK assigns cyan, magenta and yellow to those copies and multiplies their colors on white; K adds black to the ink marks. These are screen approximations, not calibrated spectral mixing or a physical emission law. Original restores the previous palette. All three modes share the same native states, phase and geometry.

Spherical coordinates

For a G9000 display point p = (x,y,z), the spherical view uses u = p / ‖p‖ and r = 1. Latitude φ = asin(uᵧ); longitude λ = atan2(u_z,uₓ). North is +Y, longitude zero is +X, and positive longitude turns toward +Z. The readouts follow the moving selected dart, in degrees. The graticule marks 30° intervals; dim lines and darts are on the far side.

The path between successive F endpoints follows a shortest great-circle arc. This is a display interpolation, not an additional native transition. Radial distance is discarded, so projected points can overlap while retaining distinct native identities. For exactly opposite points a stable great-circle plane is chosen.

The torus, sphere, bar shapes, distances, interpolated travel and playback speed are display choices. This is a native graph and transport model, not a calibrated magnetic field or a physical rotation law. The 20-step return is not a claim that a rigid magnet needs 720° to return.

The earlier signed register E[1,a,b,ab] | [ba,b,a,1]E⁻¹ remains in the original G60 view. Its signed codes and word histories are distinct from the registered endpoint permutations here; those deck maps satisfy ab = ba.
