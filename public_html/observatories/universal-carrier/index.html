About the Light Loom · Name, kernel & resources

How to read the Light Loom

Thalean G9000 · Light Loom is the name on the faceplate. The Loom is the short name. Its three strands are three views of one moving pattern. Follow the magenta band through a crossing, pause, and lower its opacity in Settings. The white at that crossing gives way to the colors carried by the other two bands. Bring magenta back and the crossing brightens again. The strands, the crossing, and the ability to pick one strand out of the weave give the instrument its name.

Thalean names the graph mechanics that supply the contacts. G9000 places the instrument in the observatory family that supplies its three-plane Flower view. The Light Loom follows one ten-contact trajectory on the underlying 60-site graph.

The same idea explains the drawers. Pause once and open Thalean, Wheeler, and Bell in turn. Thalean names a directed edge. Wheeler tells you which quotient slot its arrival site occupies. Bell gives a correlation score for the signed field at that contact. The contact number gives you a common address for three different questions.

Verify the graph, regenerate the experiment, and check the displayed pixels.

What the shared kernel does

The browser replays ten archived contacts on the finite graph AT4val[60,6], called G60 here. A dart is an edge with a direction of travel. The packet embedded in this page contains 60 sites, 120 undirected edges, and a map from the 60 sites to 15 quotient slots. Each slot collects four sites. The starting point of a comparison is the ordered pair of site numbers, written tail → head. The ten recorded darts join into a closed itinerary.

26 → 15 → 4 → 56 → 48 → 54 → 40 → 7 → 34 → 22 → 26

In the source, k is the contact index and current() selects D.rows[k]. The front panel numbers contacts 1 through 10, so contact 1 corresponds to row 0. Each row contains the dart, 20 face positions q, 20 signed velocities v, a unit-total 60-site energy profile rho, an archived potential span, four pair correlations, and the fixed-quartet CHSH score. The shared graph data live in D.edges and D.pi. Here kernel means the common replay and readout machinery. The Einstein drawer also uses the word for a specific mathematical operator, the Green kernel that produced its archived potential values.

At a contact change, render() refreshes the eight instrument summaries and the contact record from the selected index. The graph view highlights the current dart and colors sites by their recorded energy. Opening a drawer selects where that graph is shown. Between contacts, contactPhase() gives the renderer a continuous phase. Uniform pacing advances that phase evenly. Gravity pacing shapes it using archived potential samples at site 26. Both settings follow the same ten-row itinerary. Cycle speed on the top deck is measured in full cycles per second (Hz). The opening rate of 0.15 Hz is 1.5 contacts per second. At 60 Hz the clock traverses 600 contacts per second. Each frame shows the contact reached at that instant, so a fast rate can skip visible contacts or repeatedly sample a similar phase when it matches the screen refresh rate.

How a contact becomes a visible weave

The stage draws the same toroidal brightness pattern in three coordinate planes. In the default CMY palette, XZ is magenta, XY is yellow, and YZ is cyan. The displayed contact orbit makes one turn around the ring in five contacts and one around the tube in ten. The depth of a surface patch, the traveling brightness crest, and the opacities of the visible layers determine each plane’s contribution to a screen pixel. The camera determines which projected patches overlap.

With Gaussian blur enabled, the renderer keeps 35% of each plane’s projected light at its original position and spreads 65% into neighboring pixels with a Gaussian width of four CSS pixels. It then mixes the three contributions and applies master brightness. A sufficiently bright, balanced mixture reaches display white. This gives a practical reading rule. To locate a white crossing, record the camera, palette, blur, and gain. To identify the underlying contact, read the dart and its row. A change of gain can reveal a crossing while the recorded field stays fixed.

The White squelch fader beside Master brightness on the top deck tests the final eight-bit display color after scattering, palette mixing, and master brightness. At 0% the full field is visible. At higher settings a pixel passes when its red, green, and blue bytes each reach the cutoff, rounded up from 255 times the slider percentage divided by 100. Passing pixels keep their color. At 100% only RGB (255, 255, 255), or #FFFFFF, passes. Even #FFFFFE falls below the cutoff. The XYZ guide is hidden at 100% so every visible point against the stage background is a field pixel that passed. A uniformly dark stage at this setting means the current frame contains no exact white.

What each instrument reads

Reziprokium · What comes back?
The signed turn reading h · v follows the fixed turn 26 → 15 → 4 through the ten-contact sequence, using D.recip.guard[k]. Its sign reverses after five steps. The receiver-to-source panel asks a second question on the TOM-PHY-029 cover. Given the same launch, how does A’s next velocity change when B retains or clears its signed displacement? The Preserve/Swap and Retain/Clear controls select a separate two-update replay in D.recip. The outward-and-return animation follows the six-beat waltz 1–2–3 | 2–2–3. Its beat count, the ten contact rows, and the two replay updates have different meanings.
Thalean · Which contact took place?
This is the direct reading of tail and head. The archived trajectory uses a first-zero guard to select an outgoing contact. Five contact steps recover the energy profile. Ten recover the signed field and the directed contact. The optional cover sign described in this drawer has a twenty-contact return. Use the native dart when a comparison depends on which site was reached.
Einstein · What potential does the energy distribution produce?
The archived calculation applies the calibrated graph Green operator to the normalized endpoint energy. The drawer reads coarse_span, the largest potential minus the smallest. Its value is the same on all ten rows in this orbit, and the reported within-fibre response is zero. The pacing control reads another observable, the potential at the particular site 26, which varies around the orbit. A constant span and a varying local probe can therefore appear together.
Schrödinger · How is the signed face state represented?
The complex coordinate is z = q + iv. During a free interval the quadrature pair rotates by exp(−iθ). A held guard shifts the center of that evolution. The contact record exposes the first face’s position and velocity, and the packet holds all twenty face pairs. Those signed values let a reader distinguish two contacts whose endpoint energies agree.
Bohm · Which exit follows this arrival?
This drawer looks one row ahead, wrapping from the last row to the first, and displays the next arrival site. The guidance account uses the outgoing guard’s signed crossing slope. For contact 1 the current dart is 26 → 15 and the next is 15 → 4, so the guided exit reads 4. The displayed successor comes from the archived itinerary. A new initial state would require a new trajectory calculation.
Wheeler · Which part of a history survives the quotient?
The drawer evaluates D.pi[head]. Four native sites share each quotient slot. Given the slot and a declared ordering of its four lifts, two additional bits specify the arrival site. Recovering the incoming edge or a longer path requires more history. This drawer makes it possible to ask exactly which distinction was lost when four site identities became one slot.
Bohr · What do the chosen observables distinguish?
On the encoded modes, the fixed collective charge analyzers X and Z have commutator norm 2. Each single-wing mean vanishes for this preparation. The commuting Klein deck permutations act on a different register. When quoting the number 2, identify the charge analyzers and their encoded modes. The Bohr summary carries this fixed algebraic result across all contact rows.
Bell · What correlation does this preparation register?
The native pentagon quartet reads four correlations from E and an exact score from CHSH in the current row. Its score is 14/5 minus 3/2 times the energy fraction in quotient slots 1, 6, and 12. The preparation menu also offers six-axis, singlet, independent-history, routed-history, and separated-station comparisons. These use their own formulas or archived tables, including D.C and D.separated. “Register one pair” draws from the selected joint probability table. To reproduce a result, record the preparation, both analyzer settings, the contact, and whether the reported number is an exact score, a predicted probability, or a sampled count.

Reading the packet without running the animation

    
The orientation text and replay packet are part of the HTML. A reader or agent can inspect the page source without a canvas or a running clock. Save the linked HTML source as index.html and run this Python example to check the comparison directly. Use the row’s uppercase CHSH field to preserve its exact fraction. Lowercase chsh is its decimal approximation.

    
import json
from pathlib import Path

html = Path("index.html").read_text(encoding="utf-8")
payload = html.split("<script>const D=", 1)[1]
packet, _ = json.JSONDecoder().raw_decode(payload)
a, b = packet["rows"][0], packet["rows"][5]

assert a["rho"] == b["rho"]
assert a["CHSH"] == b["CHSH"] == "19119/7792"
assert packet["pi"][a["head"]] == packet["pi"][b["head"]] == 0
for field in ("q", "v"):
    assert b[field] == [-x for x in a[field]]
